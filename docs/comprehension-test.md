# One-minute portfolio comprehension test

Status: proposed acceptance test; not run. Recruit five people unfamiliar with
the projects. Do not use the builder's explanations or brief them on the products.

1. Give each person a homepage project and one minute to explore its demo.
2. Hide the page and ask: “What does this product let someone do?”
3. Ask: “What happens after their first action?”, “What did Baruch contribute?” and “What is one decision he made?”
4. Ask what was unclear, whether text was readable, and whether any replay felt
   like live AI. Note the device and whether they explored recorded moments or watched the film.
5. Repeat for the other project; alternate which project appears first.

Score each project separately. A passing answer describes the core loop and
ownership and one product decision accurately, in the participant's own words:

- SimReview: a learner interviews a helper playing a fictional patient, records
  the practice, inspects source-linked AI coaching, and picks a focus to practice
  again. Baruch designed and built the app, recording flow and feedback, using Codex
  for implementation and testing. No demonstrated learning impact is claimed.
- The City Beyond: a player describes an idea, the game judges it with reasons,
  and a successful invention has consequences in the world. Baruch defined and built the prototype with Claude Code and Codex, reviewing
  gameplay and evaluations. The portfolio
  interaction is a guided replay, not a live AI game.

Target: at least four of five people correctly explain the loop, Baruch's
contribution and one decision for each project. Record raw answers before assigning scores. Use
misunderstandings to revise the opening, captions or action sequence, then retest.
